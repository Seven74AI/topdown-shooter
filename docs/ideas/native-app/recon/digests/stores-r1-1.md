# Digest stores-r1-1: App store requirements for a wrapped Phaser co-op game (iOS + Android)

## Findings

### Apple App Review (Q1)
- Guideline 4.2: an app must "include features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website"; apps that aren't "useful, unique, or 'app-like'", or don't offer "lasting entertainment value", may be rejected. 4.2.2 bans apps that are mainly "web clippings". | https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ | Apple | undated (live page) | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | policy
- Guideline 2.5.2: apps must be "self-contained in their bundles" and may not "download, install, or execute code which introduces or changes features or functionality of the app". The only stated exception is for educational code apps. So shipping live JS updates that change game features is against the letter of 2.5.2. Bug-fix-only over-the-air updates are not explicitly addressed (that is an inference). | https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high (text); medium (inference) | policy
- Guideline 4.7 lets apps offer "HTML5 and JavaScript mini apps and mini games" that are not in the binary. This is a host-platform rule (4.7.1 to 4.7.5 require content filtering, report/block mechanisms, an index of software with universal links, and age gating). It is meant for apps that host third-party mini games, not for one developer's own wrapped game. Treating it as a route for live-updating your own game is an unverified interpretation. | https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high (text); low (applicability) | policy
- Developer reports from 2025 to 2026 show Capacitor/WebView apps getting 4.2 rejections. In one forum thread, a Capacitor app that added Core Location, reverse geocoding and clipboard access was still rejected as not different enough from web browsing. In another report, a dev publishing a PWA via Capacitor said 4.2 was the main sticking point. No documented Capacitor *game* rejection or approval was found in this run. | https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812889 ; https://scribe.disroot.org/post/scribe.disroot.org/7369014 | Apple Dev Forums; personal blog mirror | ~2025-2026 (exact date not read) | accessed 2026-11-23 | medium | process
- What makes a wrapped game pass is not evidenced by a primary source. Hypothesis: bundle all assets offline (no remote URL loading), use native-feeling UI (no browser chrome or links out), add some native integration (haptics, Game Center, share sheet), and give the reviewer demo instructions or a working server so co-op can be tested. | (no source retrieved) | — | — | accessed 2026-11-23 | low (unverified belief) | process

### Apple requirements (Q2)
- The Apple Developer Program costs 99 USD per membership year (local prices vary). The fee waiver is only for nonprofits, education and government. (Evidence comes from a search-result summary of the Apple pages; the page itself was not fetched.) | https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/ | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | medium-high | cost
- Privacy manifest: "Starting May 1, 2024, apps that don't describe their use of required reason API in their privacy manifest file aren't accepted by App Store Connect." Capacitor/WebView apps typically use UserDefaults and file-timestamp APIs (the categories are not quoted from the page; unverified). | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high (rule); medium (categories) | policy
- From May 1, 2024, new or updated apps that add an SDK from Apple's list of commonly used third-party SDKs must include that SDK's privacy manifest, required reasons, and a valid signature. Apple says required-reason enforcement "will expand to include the entire app binary". | https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=pvszzano | Apple Developer News | 2024-04-26 | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | policy
- Export compliance: "Typically, the use of encryption that's built into the operating system—for example, when your app makes HTTPS connections using URLSession—is exempt from export documentation upload requirements." Set ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = NO if the app and its libraries use only exempt encryption. HTTPS/WSS through WKWebView is likely in this category (inference). | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/complying-with-encryption-export-regulations | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high (text); medium (WSS inference) | policy
- The App Store Connect export compliance page says encryption apps distributed in France are controlled by the French government (ANSSI). The controlled categories are secure storage, secure communications, and anti-virus. A game using only HTTPS/WSS does not obviously fall into these (inference). | https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/overview-of-export-compliance | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | medium | policy
- New age ratings: 4+, 9+, 13+, 16+, 18+, with new required questions on in-app controls, capabilities, medical/wellness, and violent themes. The deadline to answer them was January 31, 2026 (otherwise update submissions are interrupted). | https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ks775ehf | Apple Developer News | 2025-07-24 | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | policy
- Account deletion (5.1.1(v)): "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app." Also: "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login." Anonymous guest play with no account creation therefore does not trigger the deletion rule (direct reading). | https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | policy
- EU DSA trader status: every developer must declare a status. Non-traders give no contact info, and EU consumers are told that consumer-protection rights don't apply. For traders, Apple publishes the verified address (or P.O. box), phone and email on the EU product page. Individuals can use a P.O. box with supporting documents. Whether you are a trader is your own legal assessment. Distributing only via TestFlight or outside the EU means "you're not acting as a trader on the App Store." | https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | policy
- Review times: "typically reviewing at least 50% of submissions in less than 24 hours and 90% in less than 48 hours." | https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/ | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | process

### TestFlight (Q3)
- TestFlight allows up to 100 internal testers, who must be App Store Connect users with access to your content, and up to 10,000 external testers, invited by email or public link. External builds may need review: "A review is required only for the first build. Subsequent builds may not require a full review." Builds can be tested for up to 90 days. | https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/test-a-beta-version/testflight-overview | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | process

### Google Play (Q4)
- Personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023 must "run a closed test for their app with a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days" before applying for production access. | https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465 | Google | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | process
- Internal testing allows "up to 100 testers per app", and these tests "might not be subject to standard Play policy or security reviews". Builds are usually available within minutes. Apps active only on internal testing tracks are exempt from the Data safety section (so a Data safety form is needed for closed/production). | https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334 | Google | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | process
- Target API: from August 31, 2026, new apps and updates must target Android 16 (API 36). Existing apps must target API 35 or higher to stay available to new users on newer OS versions. An extension to November 1, 2026 could be requested. | https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk | Google | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | policy
- Play Spam policy, "Webviews and Affiliate Spam", bans apps whose primary purpose is "to provide a webview of a website without permission from the website owner". You own the site, so the permission problem doesn't arise. A minimum-functionality rule sits in a separate "Functionality, Content, and User Experience" article, which was not read. | https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034 | Google | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high (text) | policy
- Android developer verification: limited distribution accounts launched August 2026. Enforcement started September 30, 2026 in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand on certified devices running Android 8 or higher, and expands globally in 2027. Play automatically registers 99% of Play apps. | https://developer.android.com/developer-verification | Google | undated (mentions June 2026 blog) | accessed 2026-11-23 | high | policy
- The limited distribution account is free, needs no government ID (only a Google Account, 2-Step Verification, a payments profile and a contact email), and is meant for hobbyists sharing "with family and friends". It can "share apps with up to 50 devices" via a QR-code or link handshake with user consent. Conflict: the overview page says "up to 20 devices". | https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/limited-distribution | Google | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | high (free/no ID); medium (device count) | cost/process

### Content rating (Q5)
- PEGI 7 covers "implied, non-detailed, or non-realistic violence". PEGI 12 covers "violence of a slightly more graphic nature towards fantasy characters". PEGI 16 applies when violence "looks the same as would be expected in real life". Zombies probably count as fantasy characters, so a gore-lite top-down shooter would most likely be rated PEGI 12 (inference). | https://pegi.info/what-do-the-labels-mean | PEGI | undated | accessed 2026-11-23 | medium | policy
- The Apple rating for this game is not evidenced. Hypothesis: 13+ under the new tiers, for frequent or intense cartoon/fantasy violence. | (no source retrieved) | — | — | accessed 2026-11-23 | low (unverified belief) | policy
