## 2. What went well

- **The review loop pays for itself.** Both high findings were caught before `master`: the low-RTT stutter in 2.6, which hit every snapshot on LAN, and the 2.8 merge that failed typecheck. Both re-reviews found a real new medium issue in the fixes themselves: the 0.5 s drain window caused starve/drain cycles on 4G jitter (2.4), and the dry weapon tick let a client speed up its weapon (2.7).
- **Harness-first netcode.** The netsim harness (2.6/2.7) found the fire-clock reload deviation (30–65 % reversed shots with the rifle, before the fix) and proved "server-only shot seqs 0" after the starvation fix. It is the cheapest way to verify prediction work without phones.
- **Shared rules, one implementation.** Several pieces of code are shared between server and client: `combat/cooldown.ts` (2.7), `lobbyRules.startBlocker` (2.3), `PREDICTION_SYSTEMS` (2.6/2.8) and `MUZZLE_OFFSET_M` (2.7). Wherever a rule was shared, the client and the server agreed.
- **Performance discipline held.** No review in Epic 2 found a per-tick allocation. Pools, scratch buffers and module-level callbacks are used everywhere (2.4, 2.5, 2.9).
- **Merge hygiene ideas that worked:** each story put its integration tests in its own file (`RunRoom.fight/downed/leave.test.ts`, 2.4/2.8/2.11), and builders wrote "Notes for the X merge" in their story files (2.8 → 2.6).

## 7. Epic 3 preview: dependencies and risks

**Epic 3 relies on the following from Epic 2:** `RunController` (it becomes `runStateMachine`, 2.4); the `RunPhase` union (2.1 reserved `"build"`); `syncState` / `stateView` / `snapshotWorld` / `devThrottle` field lists; `this.net.guard` for the new shop and placement messages; the 2.8 downed/out/return flow and `activeRun`; the horde record's `type+state` byte; `PREDICTION_SYSTEMS`; the 2.11 menu and edge clearing (placement mode turns the sticks off). Everything it needs exists in code. The one open dependency is 2.10's numbers, which the designer deferred.

| Risk | Why | Mitigation (decision) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R1 Flow-field cost and correctness** | 0.5 m grid on 48 × 32 m = 96 × 64 = 6,144 cells. The 3.5 AC rebuilds only on building changes, but the goal (the "nearest player") moves every tick. Runners (3.8) need a **second** field that depends on players' aim, which also changes every tick. | Decision for the 3.5/3.8 stories: a multi-source BFS/Dijkstra from the players' cells, rebuilt **incrementally on a fixed cadence** (e.g. a slice of cells per tick; a full refresh ≤ every 10 ticks) *and* on building changes. Zombies within a short line of sight steer straight at the player. The flank penalty is a cost layer refreshed on the same cadence. Benchmark with 4 players + 150 zombies + 24 buildings: **added tick cost p99 ≤ 2 ms** (today's p99 is 0.43 ms against a 50 ms tick and a 40 ms slow-tick line). The test must fail if the field goes stale for more than the cadence. |
| **R2 Buildings missing from the predictor** | Predicted movement would pass through barricades and then snap back every snapshot (2.6 note) | 3.4 adds buildings to the shared map collision that `PREDICTION_SYSTEMS` uses. Harness case: walk into a barricade at 150 ms → 0 snaps, error < 1 cm. Destroyed buildings must leave the predictor's World on the same tick they leave the mirror. |
| **R3 Schema growth vs the 6 KB/s budget** | Wallets ×4, up to 24 buildings with HP changing mid-wave, turret aim at 20 Hz, turret shots at 5/s × up to 24 turrets ≈ 120 `weapon.fired`-sized events/s (≈ 1.4 KB/s) | Turret heading is **not** synced per tick: derive it on the client from `turret.fired` events (target id). Building HP goes out on change only, quantized. Each schema story measures its patches and events with the 2.4 `tickCost` approach and reports KB/s. The whole budget (horde ≤ 24 + rest ≤ 6) is checked in 3.10 with 4 bots. |
| **R4 Phase-driven screens break again** | 2.11 [Med] and 2.8 [Med] were both "screen chosen from the wrong copy of phase". 3.1 adds `build` and a Ready button, and 3.3 adds a shop that auto-closes on `wave`. | 3.1 adds `"build"` everywhere a phase is switched on (server state machine, `main.ts` phase handler, `activeRun`, `devThrottle`, HUD) and tests each transition under `?lag`, after a hidden tab and on a late scene start. |
| **R5 Economy can't be judged if teams wipe in wave 2** | Pistol-only starts (3.3 removes the dev loadout) + 100 starting currency + SMG at 200 + the designer's decision to keep revive at 3 s / full damage | Revive numbers stay as decided (not a lever). The levers are the build-phase heal (50 %), the wave-clear bonus, prices and wave sizes (0.6/0.8 scaling). A seeded **5-wave bot sim** that buys from the shop and logs survival by wave and earnings by player is built early (A10). It is not left to 3.10's last AC. |
| **R6 Parallel merge hotspots** | Epic 3 stories all touch the run state machine, `PlayerState`/schema, the balance JSON, the World system order and the shop/HUD | Parallel plan (main session): **3.1 alone first** (state machine + phase), then {3.2 wallets, 3.4 barricade, 3.9 Crawlers} in parallel, then {3.3 shop, 3.5 flow field}, then {3.6 turret, 3.7 repair, 3.8 Runners}, and 3.10 last. Each story adds balance keys in its own JSON block and integration tests in its own `RunRoom.<story>.test.ts`. |
| **R7 Runners raise reversed hits** | 4 m/s vs Walkers; architecture: "if > 1 in 20 reversed, rewind" | The netsim harness runs a Runner-kiting case at 150 and 300 ms in 3.8 (A9). If it reaches ≥ 5 % at 150 ms, the main session creates the rewind story before 3.10. |
